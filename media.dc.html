The God of New Beginnings

Our most recent Sunday service, recorded live in Plano.

October 11, 2026 · 75 min

2 Corinthians 5:17Isaiah 43:18-19Lamentations 3:22-23

Bishop Raymond preached this message on the church’s first Sunday back in its renovated building at 3405 Custer Road. He told the church that God specializes in fresh starts, that He is the author of new beginnings, and that no one is too far gone for Him to begin again with them.

He spoke to anyone carrying the weight of past mistakes, broken relationships, unfulfilled dreams, or an uncertain season with finances, health, family or work. His encouragement was not to lose hope and not to give up on yourself, because God is not finished with you. He also pointed to the church’s own journey of leaving its building, being led elsewhere, and being brought back to a place that is now made new.

Watch on YouTube ↗

Notes from the message

  • Release the past. Isaiah 43:18 says to forget the former things. Regret and shame are in the rearview mirror, and a driver who only looks back will crash. People who stay on yesterday miss the opportunities of today.
  • Recognize what God is doing. In Isaiah 43:19 God says He is doing a new thing and asks, “Do you not perceive it?” His work often begins quietly, so we need open spiritual eyes and a willingness to cooperate. Israel kept thinking about the food of Egypt while God was leading them to a promised land.
  • The future is now. The sooner we realize it, the sooner we act to capture our destiny, instead of looking up at 85 and wondering where the time went. The enemy uses old failures to keep people from seeing their potential.
  • Be renewed in Christ. A true new beginning is a recreation by the Spirit, not turning over a new leaf in our own strength. You are no longer known as a failure, a disappointment or a confused person, because God is doing something new in your life, home, career and mind.

The Power of Unity

John 17:20-21Ephesians 4:3-6Acts 2:1

In John 17:20-21, Jesus prayed that everyone who would ever believe would be one, so that the world would know the Father sent Him. Bishop Raymond teaches that this oneness is what holds the Trinity together, and that the church is called to reflect God’s nature through love. It is not a call to uniformity, but to a one-accord relationship that overcomes division.

Read the full summary and notes

Drawing on Ephesians 4, he describes unity as a bond of peace that each person has to work at: humility, gentleness and patience, bearing with one another in love, and putting relationships ahead of opinions. He ties it to the life of this congregation, reminding everyone that it is Jesus Embassy Church, belonging to all, and not one person’s church.

Notes from the message

  • Unity reflects God, who is one and undivided. It is a sign that we are His children.
  • Unity is missional. A united church is a powerful witness to the community, while a divided one loses its impact.
  • Unity protects, like a flock of sheep. Division is the enemy’s strategy against individuals, homes and churches, and it leaves believers vulnerable.
  • Unity demands sacrifice. It asks us to set aside personal preferences for the good of the whole body. Unity is selfless, not selfish, and it sometimes takes positive compromise.
  • Unity brings power and blessing. At Pentecost the believers were together in one place and in one accord when the Holy Spirit came (Acts 2:1). Without unity, blessings leak out like money through a hole in a pocket.
  • The foundations of unity are in Ephesians 4:4-6: one body, one Spirit, one hope, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father.
  • To maintain unity, protect it actively, avoid division, and choose “we” over “me.”

April 5, 2026 · 65 min

Truths About the Resurrection

Romans 1:4Romans 6:10-11Romans 10:9John 11:25Ephesians 1:18-21Acts 4:331 Peter 1:3-41 Thessalonians 4:14

Preached on Easter Sunday, this message starts from the claim that there has never been a more powerful event than the resurrection of Jesus. Bishop Raymond says it is not just an Easter story to tell once a year. It changes things for every believer, whether or not they recognize it, and it guarantees a brighter future.

Read the full summary and notes

He walks through eight truths from Scripture, from what the resurrection proves about who Jesus is to the promise that He is coming again. Along the way he encourages the church to receive forgiveness and forgive one another, to put hope in the living Christ instead of temporary things, to use the gifts of the Spirit humbly to serve people, and to keep an eternal focus through life’s problems and stress.

Notes from the message

  • It proves who Jesus is (Romans 1:4). Only Jesus holds power over sin and death.
  • It offers the gift of forgiveness (Romans 6:10-11). Whatever sin is forgiven stays forgiven.
  • It offers salvation through Christ (Romans 10:9).
  • It gives us the ability to live freely (John 11:25), walking daily in the freedom Jesus provides.
  • It means we can have hope (Ephesians 1:18-21), because His power over the grave is a living hope.
  • It means the power of Christ can rest on us (Acts 4:33), the same power that healed the sick and drove out demons.
  • It promises eternal life in heaven with Jesus (1 Peter 1:3-4).
  • It reminds us that Jesus is coming again (1 Thessalonians 4:14). When we die, our spirit lives on in the presence of Christ.

March 22, 2026 · 71 min

The Goodness of God

Psalm 34:1-8

Based on Psalm 34:1-8, this message is about the goodness of God, which Bishop Raymond describes as who God is, not only what He does. If every item on your prayer list is not answered yet, he says, it does not change the fact that God is good. He encourages the church to choose to believe that, and to refuse worry and depression because of it.

Read the full summary and notes

He shares a testimony of a friend who was set free from alcohol and whose home and family were restored, to show that God’s help is real and available now. He closes by urging believers to be an example of that goodness in their homes, communities and workplaces, because the world is waiting to see it in them.

Notes from the message

  • God is good in His essence. Everything He made came from His goodness, including people made in His own image, so our worth is measured by God’s Word and not by our struggles.
  • God’s goodness is present every day, offering mercy and grace no matter the challenge. In the psalm, “blessed” means fortunate, prosperous and favored, for those who take refuge in Him.
  • God’s goodness leads people to repentance. It is mercy and grace, not religious works or reminders of old mistakes, that empower us to move from death to life.
  • The ultimate expression of God’s goodness is the life, death and resurrection of Jesus, who came to set us free.
  • We are called to show it. Be an example of God’s goodness to your home, community and job.

March 15, 2026 · 81 min

Living by Faith

Hebrews 11:1Hebrews 11:6James 2

Bishop Raymond opens Hebrews 11 and defines faith as the assurance of things hoped for. He compares it to the title deed to a home: because you hold the deed, you have no doubt the house is yours when someone knocks and says otherwise. Faith, he says, is conviction built on knowing God’s Word.

Read the full summary and notes

He stresses that faith has to be acted on. Quoting James, he says believing God without acting on what you believe is not biblical faith, and that many claim faith but few exercise it. Living by faith means trusting God’s promises over visible circumstances and not letting feelings decide. He holds up Abraham and Sarah, Joshua and Caleb as examples, and urges the church to learn to think and speak differently from a world that teaches doubt and fear. The service ends with prayer for the week ahead.

Notes from the message

  • Faith is assurance and conviction, the title deed to what God has promised (Hebrews 11:1).
  • Without faith it is impossible to please God (Hebrews 11:6).
  • Faith is believing plus acting. Knowledge of the Word, acted upon, brings results.
  • Abraham trusted God without doubting, even when asked to offer his son, and he and Sarah received the promise in old age.
  • Faith is a giant killer. Joshua and Caleb had a different spirit from the doubters, and Caleb could say he was as strong as he had been 40 years earlier.
  • Learn to speak the Word. Like the man who asked Jesus to “say the word,” believers can trust that God sends His word and heals.

March 8, 2026 · 86 min

Emmanuel, God With Us

Matthew 1:22-23Isaiah 7:14

From Matthew 1:22-23 and Isaiah 7:14, Bishop Raymond gives this message the title Emmanuel, which means “God with us.” The passage is often read at Christmas, he says, but God wants His people to hold on to it today, whatever they are going through.

Read the full summary and notes

He explains that Emmanuel means God is not distant but present in our suffering, our fear and our daily life, so believers are never alone. The God who was with you on the mountaintop is the same God with you in the valley, and because He does not change with your circumstances, there is no reason to be afraid of bad news. He encourages the church to focus on what the God who is with them can do through them, and to seek His presence with all their hearts.

Notes from the message

  • Emmanuel means God is literally with us. Jesus is the direct fulfillment of Isaiah’s promise, turning it into a personal reality.
  • God does not change with our circumstances. He is the same on the mountaintop and in the valley.
  • If God is with us, we do not have to fear. One person with God is the majority.
  • Jesus came to save His people from their sins, healing the separation between God and humanity.
  • Seek His presence on purpose. Those who seek Him with all their hearts will find Him.

March 1, 2026 · 78 min

Eight Reasons We Should Put Our Trust in God

Genesis 1:1Jeremiah 32:17Jeremiah 29:11Isaiah 55:10-11Psalm 139Psalm 118:6-7Romans 8:28-31

Bishop Raymond begins by admitting that trusting God can be hard when circumstances look worse or answers seem slow. He then gives eight reasons, each backed by Scripture, why God can be trusted anyway.

Read the full summary and notes

Taken together, the reasons say that the God who made everything, who holds all power, who has good plans for His people and who keeps His word is on their side, and that He works even our mistakes and hardships out for our good. The message ends with Jeremiah 17:7-8: those who trust the Lord are like a tree planted by a river, with roots that reach deep into the water.

Notes from the message

  • God created everything (Genesis 1:1, John 1:3, Romans 1:20), so He knows how all things work and has them under control.
  • Nothing is impossible with God (Jeremiah 32:17, Matthew 19:26).
  • All power belongs to Him (Job 26:14, 2 Corinthians 12:9). His power works best in our weakness.
  • God has a plan (Jeremiah 29:11, Proverbs 19:21, 1 Corinthians 2:9): plans for good, a future and a hope.
  • God keeps His word (Isaiah 55:10-11, Hebrews 6:18-19), and it is impossible for Him to lie.
  • God knows everything about us (Jeremiah 1:5, Psalm 139).
  • God is for us (Psalm 118:6-7, Romans 8:31), so we do not have to fear.
  • God works everything out for our good (Romans 8:28, Genesis 50:20, Jeremiah 17:7-8).

February 22, 2026 · 53 min · Holy Spirit Conference

Developing Intimacy With God (Part 1)

James 4:8Acts 3Matthew 7:7-8Philippians 3:10

Preached at the Holy Spirit Conference, this is the first part of a message on growing close to God. Bishop Raymond begins with a diagnosis: many churches run a lot of programs but see little change, because programs do not change lives. Only the Holy Spirit does.

Read the full summary and notes

What people need most, he says, is a stronger bond with the Spirit of God, and our greatest need is to be intimate with Him. Using James 4:8, “Come close to God and He will come close to you,” he gives the first step: if you find yourself sinning, stop and purify your heart, and do not be double-minded. He points to Philippians 3:10, wanting to know Christ and the power of His resurrection, and the service continues with ministry by the Holy Spirit.

Notes from the message

  • Stop sinning and purify your heart. Examine yourself, and do not be double-minded about it (James 4:8).
  • Ask, seek and knock (Matthew 7:7-8). God responds to people who pursue Him.
  • Jesus is the same yesterday, today and forever. In Acts 3 Peter did not only tell the man to walk, he took him by the hand.
  • Put in the effort to make time with God.
